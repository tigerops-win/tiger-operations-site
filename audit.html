02

Sample report — our own fleet

A real audit of Tiger Operations' own agent fleet, run with the same analyzer and the same two-way reconciliation a paid audit gets. This is not a customer report: no customer data appears in it, nobody paid for it, and its savings are small — stated plainly below, because that is what the numbers say.

Coverage
15 sessions, 15 log files. The logs span 7–14 October 2026; the substantive billed use is 12–14 October — the 7 October sessions are zero-cost login probes, and this sample does not present them as billed days.
Records checked
193 raw assistant records, deduplicated to 85 unique messages by message ID. 5 synthetic / zero-usage records were excluded from every total.
The dedup trap
Summing the raw records gives 131,015 output tokens. The deduplicated truth is 50,346 — a naive sum overstates output 2.6x, and any savings estimate built on it is overstated by the same factor. This check runs before any finding is priced.
Verified total
$2.7488, verified by Method B (the sessions' own cost records). Method A (per-message records priced at published list prices) gives $2.7837; every cent of the difference is itemized in the reconciliation — a cost record captured before its session's final message, one session with no cost record at all, and the hidden spend in the next row's card. Including estimates for the two defective records, the true total is an estimated $3.04–$3.18, of which $2.7488 is verified.
Cache
258,510 cache-creation tokens against 2,526,182 cache-read tokens — a 90.7% cache-read share inside sessions. Caching worked inside a session; the waste was between them: 10 of the 15 sessions lasted under 60 seconds or held two or fewer real messages, and each new session re-created its cache from scratch.
Model mix
83 of 85 messages (97.6%) ran on the top-tier model; 2 ran on the mid tier. Two of the top-tier sessions were smoke tests that produced 13 and 11 output tokens for $0.0544 and $0.0551 — almost all of each bill was creating the prompt cache on the most expensive model.

Defects are named, not hidden: one session had no cost record at all, and one had its cost record captured before its final message landed. Both are estimated from like-for-like sessions in the full reconciliation and labelled as estimates. An audit that hides its blind spots is not an audit.

///

Hidden spend

The most expensive invisible line was a Haiku web-search subagent inside one session: $0.2738 on 130,461 input tokens — 44% of that session's cost — and absent from the main session transcript. It is priced separately, line by line, exactly as a paid report prices it.

///

Agent shape

The same operation's larger fleet is subscription-billed, so the audit reports it as token volume, not cash: over 23 days, 3,720 of 3,882 agents (95.8%) produced exactly one message, and those one-shot agents consumed 1,784,108,888 of 3,291,304,374 input tokens — 54.2% of all input. Each one pays full context cost for a single message and discards it.

///

The honest verdict

Total identifiable waste in this lane: about $0.10 measured, about $0.30 at most. No monthly savings figure is extrapolated from two short project bursts — that would fabricate a run-rate. This team would not qualify for the 3x guarantee: its API-billed spend is far below the $1,000/month gate. The gate working as intended is part of the sample.

03

What you send us

Two exports covering the same period. Each one misses things the other catches; an audit on only one is incomplete.

1 · Session logs
Claude Code writes one .jsonl transcript per session — find them at ~/.claude/projects/ (macOS / Linux) or %USERPROFILE%\.claude\projects\ (Windows). Zip the whole projects folder, or just the sub-folders you want audited. Accepted formats: .zip or .tar.gz (plain .tgz and .tar also work). No sorting or renaming — the tool finds the .jsonl files inside, including in hidden folders.
2 · Billing export
Your Anthropic Console usage / billing export for the same period as the session logs. We reconcile the two against each other: logs show what your sessions did, the Console export shows what you were billed.
Never send
API keys. Passwords or credentials of any kind. Source code. .env files. The audit runs on usage exports only — none of those are needed, ever.

Transcripts include the conversation content of each session; we analyze the usage data in them — models, token counts, timing — to build your report. If you're unsure whether a file is safe to include, leave it out and ask.

The guarantee — and its gate

If we don't find $147/month in verified savings, the $49 comes back.

The 3x-savings guarantee: if you qualify, and the verified, findable savings in your report come in under $147 per month — 3x the fee — we refund the full $49. The report is yours to keep either way.

The gate: the guarantee applies to teams with at least $1,000 per month of API-billed Claude Code spend, as qualified before payment. Below that level the arithmetic stops supporting the promise for a well-run team, and we would rather gate the guarantee than sell refunds.

Below the gate? You can still buy the audit at $49 — the guarantee simply does not apply, and we tell you that before you pay, not after. The delivery terms and the unreconcileable-export refund below apply at every level.

Exports won't reconcile? If your exports cannot be reconciled with each other, we tell you why and refund the $49 in full. And if most of your usage is subscription-billed, the audit reports token patterns — it cannot promise cash savings against a flat plan, and we say so up front.
